## WhatsApp

- Document read: Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform, `https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform`, last modified 2026-09-23. The owner accepted these terms in the Meta app dashboard on 2026-11-13.
- Clause cited: section 4.7, AI Providers. Providers of large language models, generative AI platforms, general-purpose AI assistants and similar technologies may not use the platform to make those technologies available when they are the primary, rather than incidental or ancillary, functionality, as Meta decides in its sole discretion. Meta lists the countries where this use is permitted at `https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/ai-providers` (updated 2026-10-15); India is not on that list.
- Related clauses: 4.1(a), platform data must not be used to build profiles of WhatsApp users; 4.2, no legally protected data such as health information is provided to Meta; 4.7, platform data must not train AI models, except a fine-tune for the business's exclusive use.
- How Saathi is described under it: Saathi is a family helper service. On WhatsApp it carries out a fixed list of tasks for one elder: book rides, order groceries and household items, set reminders, check forwarded messages, and raise scam and emergency alerts. A caregiver approves every order. The model is the means of delivering these tasks, not the product offered, so the AI is ancillary to the service. This is the same position as any business that answers its own customers with an AI-assisted bot.
- Decision: allowed.
- Scope note, a condition of this decision: on WhatsApp, Saathi handles only its listed tasks and declines open-ended conversation or general questions, passing them to the caregiver. Saathi never presents itself on WhatsApp as a general-purpose AI assistant. If Meta objects, Telegram becomes the active elder channel as D-78 provides; the WhatsApp class stays built and tested.

## Telegram

- Document read: Telegram Bot Platform Developer Terms of Service, `https://telegram.org/tos/bot-developers`, read 2026-11-13; the page shows no version date.
- AI assistants: no clause restricts them.
- Personal data duties for the bot: a privacy policy, either Telegram's standard policy for bots or the bot's own, registered in BotFather (section 4); delete user data on request, when it is no longer needed, and when the bot stops operating (4.2); collect only what the service needs, and never build datasets or train models from it (4.3); encrypt user data at rest, with the key stored apart from it (4.4a); keep bot credentials private (4.5).
- Retention and encryption on Telegram's side: Telegram Privacy Policy, `https://telegram.org/privacy`, section 3.3.1. Bot conversations are cloud chats, which Telegram stores on its servers, encrypted, with the keys held in other data centres. Section 6.3 lists what a bot receives: the user's public profile, the messages sent to it, and the interface language.
